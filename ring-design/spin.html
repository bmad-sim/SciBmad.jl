15. Spin Tracking with Ramping¶

Introduction¶

SciBmad computes the most important quantities of polarization analysis, such as the invariant spin field and the amplitude-dependent spin tune (with twiss(…; spin=true), see Twiss), and tracks spins (with track(…; spin=true)). In this chapter, we will ramp the energy of a storage ring through a spin-orbit resonance, calculate the polarization transmission, and compare it with the resonance strength computed from the invariant spin field, with the Froissart-Stora formula.

In a perfectly flat ring (a ring in which the only magnetic fields on the closed orbit are vertical), the closed-orbit spin tune is \(\nu_0 = G\gamma\), where \(G = (g-2)/2\) is the anomalous gyromagnetic \(g\)-factor and \(\gamma\) the Lorentz factor of the reference particle. When \(\nu_0\) is close to a frequency of the orbital motion, \(\kappa = k_0 + k_1 Q_1 + k_2 Q_2\) with integers \(k_i\), the spin motion is driven resonantly. Each turn, the spin is rotated a little around a direction that depends on the orbit, and the rotations add up. We will ramp our ring through the resonance \(\nu_0 = 60 - Q_2\).

  1. The resonance strength \(\epsilon\) is computed from the invariant spin field \(\hat{n}\) (see Twiss). Near an isolated resonance, at a distance \(\Delta = \nu_0 - \kappa\) from it, \(\hat{n}\) for a particle with an orbital amplitude is tilted away from its direction \(\hat{n}_0\) on the closed orbit by the angle \(\theta\) with \(\sin\theta = \epsilon/\sqrt{\epsilon^2 + \Delta^2}\), where \(\epsilon\) is proportional to the amplitude. So for \(|\Delta| \gg \epsilon\), \(\epsilon = |\Delta|\,|\hat{n} - \hat{n}_0|\).

    Set \(G\gamma = \kappa + 0.05\), and compute the invariant spin field as a Taylor series in the phase space coordinates (as_taylor_series=true, to first order) at the start of the ring:

Δ = 0.05
ring.E_ref = (κ + Δ)/G*m
twn = twiss(ring, spin=true, order=1, as_taylor_series=true, at=[1],
            cols=["nx", "ny", "nz", "beta2", "alpha2"])

The derivatives of \(\hat{n}\) with respect to \(y\) and \(p_y\) are coefficients of the Taylor series: t[[0,0,1,0,0,0]] is the coefficient of \(y\). A particle with the vertical action \(J_2\) is at \(y = \sqrt{2J_2\beta_2}\cos\phi\), \(p_y = -\sqrt{2J_2/\beta_2}\,(\sin\phi + \alpha_2\cos\phi)\). Its action is that of the tracked particle, which started at \(y = y_0\), \(p_y = 0\). The tilt of \(\hat{n}\), averaged over the phase \(\phi\):

β2, α2 = scalar(twn.beta2[1]), scalar(twn.alpha2[1])
J2 = y0^2*(1 + α2^2)/(2β2)
n = [twn.nx[1], twn.ny[1], twn.nz[1]]
dn_dy = [t[[0,0,1,0,0,0]] for t in n]
dn_dpy = [t[[0,0,0,1,0,0]] for t in n]
tilt = [sqrt(sum(abs2, dn_dy .* (sqrt(2J2*β2)*cos(ϕ)) .-
                       dn_dpy .* (sqrt(2J2/β2)*(sin(ϕ) + α2*cos(ϕ)))))
        for ϕ in range(0, 2pi, 37)[1:36]]
ϵ = Δ*sum(tilt)/36

0.0005958629526739844

  1. The Froissart-Stora formula gives the vertical spin long after crossing an isolated resonance with a constant speed, for a spin that was vertical long before:

    \[ S_y(\theta \to \infty) = 2\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\epsilon^2}{2\tilde{\alpha}}\right) - 1\]

    Here \(\theta\) is the machine azimuth, \(\epsilon\) is the resonance strength, and \(\tilde{\alpha} = \frac{d}{d\theta} G\gamma = \frac{1}{2\pi}\times[\text{change in } G\gamma \text{ per turn}]\).

α̃ = 0.2/n_turns/(2pi)
2*exp(-pi*ϵ^2/(2α̃)) - 1

0.8322440062521066

This agrees with the tracking above to the accuracy of the approximations: the resonance is crossed from a finite distance, and \(\epsilon\) was computed at a finite distance from the resonance.

Exercises¶

  1. Siberian snakes: The AGS has two partial Siberian snakes, which rotate the spin around the longitudinal axis: the cold snake (at csnk) by \(18.3^\circ\) and the warm snake (at wsnk) by \(10.57^\circ\). Model them as point-like rotations of the spin, with a transport_map (see MapParams) that rotates the spin quaternion q by the unit quaternion \((\cos(\vartheta/2), \sin(\vartheta/2)\,\hat{e})\), which rotates the spin by the angle \(\vartheta\) around the unit vector \(\hat{e}\). Compute the spin tune at different energies with twiss(ring, spin=true), and verify that it is not the fractional part of \(G\gamma\). Why is this the case? (Without the snakes, set the maps back to nothing.)

  2. Resonance spectrum: Compute the resonance strengths \(\epsilon\) as above for the resonances \(\kappa = N \pm Q_2\) with \(G\gamma\) between 30 and 60, for the same vertical amplitude. What do you observe about the spectrum? Can you explain any of its features? Hint: look at the structure of the lattice.

  3. Crossing speed: Track through the resonance with different numbers of turns, from 100 to 10000, and compare the final \(S_y\) with the Froissart-Stora formula.
