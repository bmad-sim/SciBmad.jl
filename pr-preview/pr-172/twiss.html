Twiss¶

twiss is a general, all-encompassing function for doing analysis of any beamline, open or closed, via perturbation theory around the (closed) orbit. It computes the linear and nonlinear optics (e.g. amplitude-dependent tunes and resonance driving terms) and, with spin=true, the invariant spin field and the amplitude-dependent spin tune. The dependence of all of these on the relative momentum deviation δ (the coordinate pz) and on parameters of the lattice (magnet strengths, lengths, etc) can also be computed.

Basics¶

All twiss needs is a beamline:

@elements begin
  qf = Quadrupole(Kn1=0.36, L=0.5)
  d = Drift(L=1.0)
  qd = Quadrupole(Kn1=-0.36, L=0.5, n_steps=2)
  b = SBend(L=2.0, angle=pi/20)
end
fodo = Beamline([qf, d, b, d, qd, d, b, d], species_ref=Species("electron"), pc_ref=18e9)
tw = twiss(fodo)

The output is a Twiss structure. Shown first are the summary values, which describe the motion through the whole beamline: in this case, the tunes q1 and q2, the slip factor etac and the momentum compaction alphac. Then follows a table of the s-dependent quantities, with one row per location: by default, every integration step through every element.

If no initial lattice functions are provided (see Open lines), twiss finds the closed orbit and computes the periodic solution. The map once through the beamline is computed as a Taylor series in the deviations of the six phase space coordinates from the closed orbit, using high-order automatic differentiation, and is then brought to normal form. The normalizing map is the change of coordinates in which the motion is a rotation in each mode (a damped one with radiation damping): mode 1 (horizontal-like), mode 2 (vertical-like) and, with RF cavities (synchrotron motion), mode 3 (longitudinal-like). The normalizing map is then tracked through the beamline, and the lattice functions at each location are computed from it.

To first order, the default, the beta and alpha functions (Twiss parameters), the phase advances, the dispersion, the coupling along the beamline, and the tunes are computed. Summary values and columns carry the number of their mode, e.g. q1 the horizontal-like tune and beta2 the vertical-like beta function. Coupled motion is described in the Sagan-Rubin/Edwards-Teng formalism by default.

The summary values are properties of Twiss:

Expressions for matching and indexing a Beamline use the same rules as above.

A column can also be used as a whole, as the vector of all rows of the table. For example:

using CairoMakie
lines(tw.s, tw.beta1)       # the beta function along the beamline
maximum(tw.beta1)           # 14.56
tw.dx ./ sqrt.(tw.beta1)    # [1.059, 1.059, ...] at every row

The underlying vector of a TwissColumn can be obtained with collect(tw.beta1). The whole table, here tw.df is a DataFrame.

Choosing rows and columns¶

at chooses the rows and cols the columns:

tws = twiss(fodo, at=[qd], cols=["beta1", "dx"])
tws.beta1[qd] # 6.695

6.695347129538178

at is a vector of elements (the beginning of every placement), beamline indexes and names (the beginning of the element), and s-ranges (every integration step in the range). Only these locations can then be read, and computing fewer rows is faster. With at=[] only the summary values are computed:

twiss(fodo, at=[]).q1 # 0.1486522740866844

0.1486522740866844

A table of only the orbit (and, without RF cavities, the dispersion) is faster too. Besides the default columns, cols can ask for these (all are listed in the twiss docstring at the end of this page):

Chromatic and amplitude-dependent quantities¶

Beyond first order, the Taylor series give the dependence of the optics on δ (chromatic quantities) and the nonlinear motion: amplitude-dependent tunes and resonance driving terms. For example the chromaticities, the first derivatives of the tunes with respect to δ, need the expansion to second order in δ, which the keyword argument chrom sets:

twc = twiss(fodo, chrom=2)
twc.dq1, twc.dq2 # (-0.10506231945773534, -0.1180234082730797)

(-0.10506231945773513, -0.11802340827307978)

dq1 is the first derivative of q1 with respect to δ. This naming works for any quantity: d<quantity>_<n> is its n-th derivative with respect to δ, and _1 can be left out. The dispersion dx is itself the derivative of the orbit x. Derivatives of columns are asked for in cols, e.g. the second derivative of x (the second-order dispersion) and the first derivative of beta1:

twd = twiss(fodo, chrom=2, cols=["dx_2", "dbeta1"])
twd.dx_2[qd], twd.dbeta1[qd] # (4.366, 0.7423)

(4.365559964845147, 0.7422943146852912)

How far the expansion goes is set by two truncation orders: chrom for δ alone, and order for all six phase space coordinates (chrom defaults to order).

The orbit and the dispersion are known to the order of the expansion in δ. The other quantities (tunes, β, α, phase advances, …) come from the first-order terms of the map, so they are known to one order less in δ. With chrom=2, for example, the dispersion is known to second order in δ (dx_2), but the tunes only to first order (the chromaticities).

The expansion in δ needs a beam without synchrotron motion, in which δ stays constant. For a beamline with RF cavities, also give rf_on=false, which turns the cavities off (see Synchrotron motion and radiation).

With chrom or order of 2 or more, the tunes are not numbers but Taylor series, and indexing them gives their derivatives:

tw3 = twiss(fodo, chrom=3)
tw3.q1

AmplitudeDependentValue:
 0.14865227408668438 - 0.1050623194577349 δ + 1.6521679715922206 δ²

tw3.q1[delta=2] # 3.304

3.3043359431844412

These AmplitudeDependentValues are Taylor series in δ and in the actions J₁ and J₂, which measure the amplitudes of modes 1 and 2. Indexing gives a derivative: tw3.q1[delta=2] is \(\partial^2 q_1/\partial\delta^2\), which can also be written tw3.dq1_2. For the coefficient of δ² as it is printed, i.e. the derivative divided by 2!, use getterm(tw3.q1, delta=2).

Derivatives with respect to the actions need order:

twiss(fodo, order=3).q1[J1=1] # 0.6817, ∂q1/∂J1

0.6816882828855374

The truncation orders that some quantities need:

Quantity

Needs

dq1 (q1[delta=1]), dbeta1, dx_2

dq1_2 (q1[delta=2]), dbeta1_2, dx_3

In general, an n-th derivative with respect to δ needs chrom of at least n+1 (for the orbit and the dispersion, n). With derivatives with respect to the actions too, it needs order of at least n+1, plus 2 for each of them. Asking for a derivative that the truncation does not resolve is an error that says which order is needed.

Without order and chrom, twiss uses the lowest orders that the requested columns need: twiss(fodo, cols=["dx_2"]) uses chrom=2, and twiss(fodo, cols=["h3000"]) order=2. Summary values are not columns, so for the chromaticities alone give chrom, as above.

The maps can also be expanded in deviations of parameters of the lattice, e.g. a magnet strength: all of these quantities are then Taylor series in the parameters too, see Parametric Normal Form.

Synchrotron motion and radiation¶

With RF cavities, the longitudinal motion oscillates too, and twiss computes it as mode 3:

@elements begin
  cav = RFCavity(L=0.1, voltage=5e6, harmon=15, zero_phase=PhaseRef.AboveTransition)
end
fodorf = Beamline([qf, d, b, d, qd, d, b, d, cav], species_ref=Species("electron"),
                  pc_ref=18e9)
twr = twiss(fodorf)
twr.q3 # -0.008711

-0.008711489063323393

The synchrotron tune q3 is negative above transition, as here, and positive below it, because the longitudinal motion turns the other way in the z-pz plane.

The table now also has the longitudinal phase advance phi3 and the damping decrements dampdec1, dampdec2, dampdec3 from the start of the table. These are zero here, as there is no radiation damping. Radiation damping is turned on per element with rad_damping=true (see Integration Steps and Slices), and then also gives the summary values damp1, damp2, damp3.

As the chromatic quantities need a beam without synchrotron motion, twiss(fodorf, chrom=2) is an error. twiss(fodorf, chrom=2, rf_on=false) computes them with the RF cavities turned off.

Spin¶

With spin=true, twiss also computes the spin motion:

twsp = twiss(fodo, spin=true)
twsp.qspin

AmplitudeDependentValue:
 -0.042444434426474775 - 2.0424444326998827 δ

The spin tune qspin is a Taylor series in δ here. With only horizontal bends, it is \(-a\gamma\,\theta/2\pi\) up to a whole number, where θ = π/10 is the total bending angle and aγ = 40.85 for 18 GeV electrons (\(a\) is the anomalous magnetic moment). It changes with δ as aγ does.

The table now also has n0x, n0y, n0z: the spin direction on the closed orbit that repeats itself after each pass (here vertical: twsp.n0y[qd] is −1). The invariant spin field extends this direction to the phase space around the closed orbit: a spin along the field stays along the field, at the particle’s new position, after each pass. The columns dnx, dny, dnz are its derivatives with respect to δ.

Open lines¶

An open line, such as a transfer line, has no closed orbit. Instead, the orbit and the lattice functions at its start are given with initial, and the normalizing map at the start is built from them:

@elements begin
  qa = Quadrupole(Kn1=0.36, L=0.5)
  qb = Quadrupole(Kn1=-0.36, L=0.5)
  qc = Quadrupole(Kn1=0.2, L=0.5)
end
line = Beamline([qa, d, qb, d, qc, d], species_ref=Species("electron"), pc_ref=18e9)
twl = twiss(line, initial=(beta1=10.0, alpha1=-1.0, beta2=5.0, alpha2=0.5, dx=0.2))
twl.beta1[end] # 12.30

12.297164887596177

The values are given by the names of the columns: the Twiss parameters beta1 and beta2 (required), alpha1 and alpha2, the dispersion dx, dpx, dy, dpy, the coupling c11, c12, c21, c22, and the orbit x, px, y, py, z, pz (the others are zero if not given). initial can also be a Dict (for example a Python dictionary), or a row of another table, e.g. to continue the periodic lattice functions of a cell into a line that starts with it:

cell = Beamline([qa, d, qb, d], species_ref=Species("electron"), pc_ref=18e9)
twcell = twiss(cell)
twiss(line, initial=twcell.df[1, :], cols=["beta1", "alpha1", "beta2", "alpha2"])

A part of a beamline¶

The keyword arguments start and stop restrict the analysis to a part of the beamline, e.g. a single cell of a line of several cells, as a periodic beamline by itself:

cells = Beamline([qf, d, b, d, qd, d, b, d, qf, d, b, d, qd, d, b, d],
                 species_ref=Species("electron"), pc_ref=18e9)
twp = twiss(cells, start=9) # the second cell
twp.q1, twp.beta1[begin]    # (0.1487, 14.56), as for fodo

(0.1486522740866844, 14.558553433187578)

The part goes from the beginning of the element start to the beginning of stop (by default the end of the beamline). Its table is read like that of the whole beamline, except that:

  • twp.beta1[begin] and twp.beta1[end] are the beginning and the end of the part;

  • locations with several values (:, an s-range, …) give only those in the part;

  • an element must be placed once in the part.

s and the beamline indexes stay those of the whole beamline, and the phases are counted from start.

With initial or a_initial, the part is an open line that starts at start. For example, from lattice functions given at the beginning of the second cell to the beginning of its qd (beamline index 13):

two = twiss(cells, start=9, stop=13, initial=(beta1=10.0, beta2=5.0))
two.beta1[end] # 2.345

2.3453307638692276

Resonance driving terms and detuning (h columns)¶

For a periodic beamline, the columns h<ijkl> (without synchrotron motion) and h<ijklmn> (with it) are the coefficients of the nonlinear effective Hamiltonian \(h\), e.g. in a cell of thin quadrupoles with a thin sextupole:

tqf = Multipole(Kn1L=0.8)
tqd = Multipole(Kn1L=-0.7)
sx = Multipole(Kn2L=0.01)
thincell = Beamline([tqf, d, sx, tqd, d], species_ref=Species("electron"), pc_ref=1e9)
twh = twiss(thincell, cols=["beta1", "phi1", "h3000", "h2100"])
twh.h3000[1] # 0.0002407 - 0.001088im at the start

0.00024074396998282022 - 0.0010882445118449418im

In normalized coordinates, one pass through the beamline from the row back to it is the motion under \(h\) for one unit of time, followed by the rotation by the tunes. In the operator notation of the Quickstart,

\[\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{A}^{-1}\exp{(:h:)}\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathcal{A}\]

where \(\mathcal{M}\) is the map around the closed orbit once through the beamline from the row back to it, \(\mathcal{A}\) the linear (Courant-Snyder) normalizing map at the row and \(\mathcal{R}\) the rotation by the tunes (with their dependence on δ and on parameters). \(h\) is a polynomial in the complex variables

\[z_{2k-1} = \frac{\tilde{x}_k + i\tilde{p}_k}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{J_k}\,e^{-i\phi_k}, \qquad z_{2k} = \frac{\tilde{x}_k - i\tilde{p}_k}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{J_k}\,e^{i\phi_k},\]

where \(\tilde{x}_k\), \(\tilde{p}_k\) are the normalized coordinates of mode \(k\), \(J_k\) its action and \(\phi_k\) its angle. (\(\mathcal{A}\) takes the normalized coordinates to the laboratory coordinates: without coupling and dispersion, \(\tilde{x}_1 = \sqrt{2J_1}\cos\phi_1\) and \(x = \sqrt{2\beta_1 J_1}\cos\phi_1\).) In these variables,

\[h = \sum h_{ijklmn}\, z_1^i z_2^j z_3^k z_4^l z_5^m z_6^n ,\]

and \(\exp(:h:)\) is the motion given by Hamilton’s equations \(d\tilde{x}_k/dt = \partial h/\partial\tilde{p}_k\), \(d\tilde{p}_k/dt = -\partial h/\partial\tilde{x}_k\).

The terms with equal exponents of \(z_{2k-1}\) and \(z_{2k}\) for every mode (e.g. h2200) change the tunes with the amplitudes (detuning). The others drive resonances. The column h<ijklmn> needs order of at least \(i+j+k+l+m+n-1\).

To first order in their strengths, thin multipoles add their Hamiltonians to \(h\). For a normal multipole of order \(n\) (Kn<n>L) this is \(K_nL\,\mathrm{Re}\,(x+iy)^{n+1}/(n+1)!\), with \(x = \sqrt{\beta_1/2}\,(z_1e^{-i\varphi_1} + z_2e^{i\varphi_1})\) and \(y = \sqrt{\beta_2/2}\,(z_3e^{-i\varphi_2} + z_4e^{i\varphi_2})\). Here \(\beta_k\) are the beta functions at the multipole and \(\varphi_k\) the phase advances (in radians) from the row to the multipole, for uncoupled motion without dispersion at the multipole. For example:

  • a thin sextupole gives \(h_{3000} = K_2L\,\beta_1^{3/2}e^{-3i\varphi_1}/(12\sqrt{2})\), \(h_{2100} = 3K_2L\,\beta_1^{3/2}e^{-i\varphi_1}/(12\sqrt{2})\) and \(h_{1020} = -K_2L\,\beta_1^{1/2}\beta_2\,e^{-i(\varphi_1+2\varphi_2)}/(4\sqrt{2})\);

  • a thin octupole gives \(h_{2200} = K_3L\,\beta_1^2/16\) and \(h_{4000} = K_3L\,\beta_1^2e^{-4i\varphi_1}/96\).

For the sextupole above:

φ = 2pi*twh.phi1[sx]

twh.h2100[1], 3*0.01*β^1.5/(12*sqrt(2))*cis(-φ)  # the same for h2100

Reference¶

twiss — Function.

twiss(bl::Beamline; kwargs...)

General function to compute the (parametric and nonlinear) optics of a Beamline. Returns a Twiss struct that contains the (amplitude-dependent) (spin) tunes/slip, and a dataframe of the lattice functions at each specified integration step. By default, lattice functions of the Sagan-Rubin/Edwards-Teng coupling formalism are computed in the dataframe at every integration step. The columns are read at locations of the beamline, e.g. tw.beta1[qd] at the beginning of the element qd (see TwissColumn), and the whole table is tw.df.

The cols keyword argument can be set to customize what quantities will be computed and stored as columns in the returned dataframe. E.g.,

tw = twiss(bl; cols=["beta1"])

will compute only the horizontal-like beta function. We can also specify so-called “resonance driving terms”/”detune coefficients” of the nonlinear effective Hamiltonian,

tw = twiss(bl; cols=["beta1", "h3000"], order=2)

where the total order of the twiss call has also been set appropriately to compute h3000 (without order and chrom, the orders that the columns need are chosen).

An arbitrary order chromatic derivative of any scalar-valued column can be taken using the syntax d<column>_<order>. If order==1, then the _1 can be omitted. E.g., the linear and 2nd order dispersions, as well as ∂β₁/∂δ can be computed with

tw = twiss(bl; cols=["dx", "dx_2","dbeta1"], chrom=2)

where the chrom keyword argument, which specifies solely the truncation order of δ, has been set appropriately.

Chromatic derivatives need a beam without synchrotron motion: rf_on=false if there are RF cavities.

To customize the output, such as computing higher order (chromatic) lattice functions, spin lattice functions/tune, amplitude-dependent tunes, and parametric quantities, see the keyword arguments:

Keyword arguments

  • at::Union{Colon, AbstractVector}: Where to compute the lattice functions (the rows of the table): a vector of locations, each as one index of a column (see TwissColumn): elements (at the beginning of each placement), beamline indexes, names (as for findelements), and s-ranges (tuples). Default :, every integration step and the end of the beamline. An entry that is not in the beamline is an error; in a part of the beamline (start, stop) only the locations in the part are computed.

  • start, stop: Elements (or their beamline indexes) to compute the optics of only a part of the beamline: from the beginning of start (default: the first element) to the beginning of stop (default: the end of the beamline). Without initial or a_initial these are the periodic optics of that part by itself (e.g. of one cell of a ring), with them those of an open line that starts at start. s and index in the table are those of the whole beamline, phases are counted from start, and the last row is the beginning of stop.

  • in_body_coordinates::Bool: If true the lattice functions inside misaligned elements are not transformed back to the reference curve coordinates. Default is false

  • rf_on::Bool: If false, then any RFParams in any LatElements are ignored for the entirety of the Twiss tracking and calculation. Default is true

  • order::Integer: TPSA truncation order for all phase space coordinates used in the Twiss calculation. If neither order nor chrom is given, both are chosen from cols (as TwissExpr does): 1 for the default columns, higher for chromatic derivatives (chrom) and resonance driving terms (order). Default is 1 if only chrom is given

  • chrom::Integer: TPSA truncation order for only δ, default is order if order is given

  • GTPSA_descriptor::Union{Descriptor,Nothing}: Custom GTPSA Descriptor to use for the Twiss calculation, required to provide to do parametric normal form and lattice function calculations. Default is Descriptor([order, order, order, order, order, chrom], max(order,chrom)).

  • initial: The lattice functions at the start of an “open” line (e.g. a transfer line), by name: beta1, beta2 (required), alpha1, alpha2, the coupling c11, c12, c21, c22, the dispersion dx, dpx, dy, dpy, and the orbit x, px, y, py, z, pz (all 0 if not given; pz is δ, else delta0), with the meanings of the columns of the same names. A NamedTuple, a Dict (with Symbol or String keys), a Context (whose variables can then be knobs of a match), or a row of another table (tw.df[end, :]; its other columns are ignored). The values may be Taylor series in GTPSA parameters, not in the phase space variables or δ. The beam has no synchrotron motion: RF cavities with voltage need rf_on=false (or a 6D a_initial). Default is nothing, to compute the periodic (“closed”) lattice functions

  • a_initial::Union{DAMap,Nothing}: A DAMap of the initial transformation from normal form coordinates to laboratory coordinates, to compute the lattice functions of an “open” line (instead of initial). A coasting a_initial (without synchrotron motion) also needs rf_on=false if the line has RF cavities with voltage. Default is nothing

  • damping::Union{Bool,Nothing}: Specifies if radiation damping is included. Default is nothing, which will auto-detect from a_initial or the periodic a.

  • delta0: If the beam is coasting (no longitudinal oscillations), then the optics will be computed around this delta-dependent orbit. Else, this will be an initial guess for the 6D closed orbit calculation. Default is 0.. If a_initial is provided then this will be ignored, UNLESS a_initial is coasting, in which case this will set the value for δ. With initial, it is δ if initial has no pz.

  • v0: Initial guess for the closed orbit finder if a_initial is not provided, else setting this will override the orbit expansion origin coordinates in a_initial.v0. Not with initial, which gives the orbit.

  • spin::Bool: If true, spin is included in the Twiss calculation. Default is false, or true if a_initial includes spin.

  • base_cols: A vector of “base columns”, that will always be included in the Twiss dataframe regardless of what is specified in cols. Defaults to the global variable SciBmad.base_cols, which defaults to ["index", "name", "kind", "s"].

  • cols: A vector of columns to include in the Twiss dataframe. See the extended help for all possible columns.

  • as_taylor_series::Union{Bool,Nothing}: If true, the lattice functions in the dataframe are returned as Taylor series types - this will be necessary if doing parametric normal form. Default is nothing, which autoselects to false if there are no parameters in the GTPSA, and true if there are parameters.

  • coast_tol: Tolerance for the first-order delta dependences on each of the phase space variables below which coasting beam is assumed, passed to find_closed_orbit. Default is 1e-14.

  • symplectic_tol::Float64: Tolerance of symplectic condition violation, above which radiation damping is assumed. Default is 1e-8.

  • batch_index::Int: If any element parameters are BatchParam, sets which index in the batch the twiss should be evaluated at. Default is 1. Batched element lengths and a batched reference energy are not supported in twiss yet.

To see a description of all quantities available to include in cols, see the extended help section using ??twiss

TwissColumn — Type.

TwissColumn

A column of a Twiss table (e.g. tw.beta1), indexed with locations of the beamline exactly as the columns of a TwissExpr:

  • tw.beta1[qd], tw.beta1[5]: at the beginning of the element qd (one value; qd must be placed once in the beamline, else it is an error), or of the element with beamline index 5. Elements made from qd (copies, also of copies) are placements of qd

  • tw.beta1[[qd]]: at the beginning of every placement of qd, in beamline order (a vector)

  • tw.beta1[begin], tw.beta1[end]: at the beginning and the end of the beamline (or of the part of it given with start and stop). As an index, end is the beamline index of the end (one more than the number of elements, or the index of stop), so tw.beta1[end-1] is at the beginning of the last element

  • tw.beta1[5, :exit], tw.beta1[qd, :exit]: at the end of the element (the beginning of the next one). The end of the beamline is like a zero-length element: [end], [end, :entrance] and [end, :exit] are the same location

  • tw.beta1[5, :], tw.beta1[qd, :]: at every integration step of the element, both ends included (a vector)

  • tw.beta1[5, 2], tw.beta1[qd, 2]: two integration steps after the beginning of the element

  • tw.beta1["q*"], tw.beta1[r"..."], tw.beta1[[qf, qd]], tw.beta1[2:5]: at the beginning of the elements found by name (as for findelements), or of each element given (a vector). With a position, e.g. tw.beta1[[qd], :exit], at that position of each

  • tw.beta1[(2.0, 10.0)], tw.beta1[:]: at every integration step in an s-range (and the end of the beamline if the range contains it), or of the whole beamline (a vector)

In the table of a part of the beamline (start, stop), a vector has the locations in the part only, an element must be placed once in the part, and a beamline index outside it is an error. The table must have rows at these locations (it has with the default at). The column also iterates over all rows of the table, in order, and broadcasts (tw.dbeta1 ./ tw.beta1) as a vector of them; collect(tw.beta1) (or tw.df.beta1) is that vector, and first and last are its first and last rows.
