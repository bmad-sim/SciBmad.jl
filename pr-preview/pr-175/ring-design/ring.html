5. Constructing the Ring¶

Ring Construction¶

In the previous chapters, forward and reverse arc cells, forward and reverse straight section cells, and forward and reverse lines to connect the arcs to the straight sections (with dispersion suppression and matching of the beta functions) were constructed. In this chapter, the entire ring is created, based on the figure above. All that is left is a section to match the straight section to the arc, including dispersion “creation” and matching of the beta functions, as shown in the figure of a sextant below.

Does this mean that a dispersion creator for both the forward and reverse sextants needs to be matched? Fortunately, no! Considering the mirror symmetry shown above, the quadrupole settings for the forward sextant dispersion suppression and matching are equal to the quadrupole settings for the reverse dispersion creation and matching. Likewise, the reverse dispersion suppression and matching quadrupole settings are equal to those of the forward dispersion creation and matching. Therefore, no matching is necessary here. All that is needed is to connect the lines together.

Example: Forward Straight Section to Arc¶

Start with the lattice of chapter 3:

include("lattices/03-match-to-straight.jl");

As an example, the forward line to connect the straight section to the arc, ss_to_arcf, will be constructed. Following the figure of the sextant, both the matching section to the dispersion creator (mdcf) and the dispersion creator itself (dispcref) need to be constructed. By the mirror symmetry, ss_to_arcf uses the same quadrupoles as arc_to_ssr, in reverse order. Keeping with our convention of starting each line with a full horizontally focusing quadrupole, this is

# Match the forward straight section to the dispersion creator (reverse quadrupoles)
mdcf = [qfss, d1, db, d2, qdr3, d1, db, d2, qfr3, d1, db, d2, qdr2, d1, db, d2]

# Forward dispersion creator (reverse quadrupoles)
dispcref = [qfr2, d1, bh, d2, qdr1, d1, bh, d2, qfr1, d1, bh, d2, qd, d1, bh, d2]

ss_to_arcf = [mdcf; dispcref];

Note that the elements in a forward line keep the forward order of the drifts (d1 after a quadrupole, d2 before it); only the quadrupoles come from the reverse sextant.

After matching the straight sections to the arcs, it is now time to construct the full ring. The reverse line ss_to_arcr is constructed in the same way (exercise 1). In Julia, repeat(fodoaf, 20) repeats the cell 20 times, and [a; b; c] joins the lines a, b and c:

sextant1 = [repeat(fodossf, 4); ss_to_arcf; repeat(fodoaf, 20); arc_to_ssf; repeat(fodossf, 4)]
sextant3 = [repeat(fodossr, 4); ss_to_arcr; repeat(fodoar, 20); arc_to_ssr; repeat(fodossr, 4)]
sextant5 = [repeat(fodossf, 4); ss_to_arcf; repeat(fodoaf, 20); arc_to_ssf; repeat(fodossf, 4)]
sextant7 = [repeat(fodossr, 4); ss_to_arcr; repeat(fodoar, 20); arc_to_ssr; repeat(fodossr, 4)]
sextant9 = [repeat(fodossf, 4); ss_to_arcf; repeat(fodoaf, 20); arc_to_ssf; repeat(fodossf, 4)]
sextant11 = [repeat(fodossr, 4); ss_to_arcr; repeat(fodoar, 20); arc_to_ssr; repeat(fodossr, 4)]

ring = Beamline([sextant1; sextant3; sextant5; sextant7; sextant9; sextant11],
                species_ref=electron, pc_ref=pc_ref)

The beta functions are periodic in the cells of the arcs and of the straight sections, and the dispersion is zero in the straight sections:

maximum(abs, tw.dx[1:33]) # Through the first straight section: the elements 1 to 32 [m]

1.1655636589206915e-14

Here 1:33 are beamline indexes: the beginnings of the elements 1 to 32 of the ring, and of the element 33, which is the end of the straight section.

Exercises¶

  1. Reverse straight section to arc: Construct ss_to_arcr, using the example above and the figures of the ring and of the sextant.

  2. Construct the ring: Construct the ring yourself from the lines of the previous chapters, as above. What are its tunes? Why are they not multiples of the phase advances of the cells?

  3. Check that the ring closes geometrically: The sum of the bend angles must be \(2\pi\): sum(ele.g_ref*ele.L for ele in ring.line). Compute also the global coordinates at the end of the ring, with the loop of Machine Coordinates: they must be those at the beginning. What is the circumference of the ring?

  4. Floor plan: Plot the global coordinates \(Z\) against \(X\) at the exit ends of all elements, to draw the floor plan of the ring. Can you see the six arcs and straight sections? Draw the floor plan of one sextant.

The lattice file of this chapter is lattices/05-ring.jl.
